# bread

Basic Bread Recipe by weight:
* Flour 100%
* Water 65-85%
* Salt 2%
* Yeast 0.5%
    * Or 20% Natural Leaven

## Bakers percentages
Percentages are used so that the it can be scaled up or down. Weights are used to provide a consistent baking experience.
Volumetric measures such as cups depend on how dense the material is, see [website](https://www.loveandoliveoil.com/2020/01/weight-vs-volume-measurements-in-baking-and-the-best-way-to-measure-flour.html)
for more details on why to use weight when measuring flour. For salt, a teaspoon of a fine salt like table salt is
actually much saltier than a teaspoon of a coarser salt like kosher salt. 

All percentages are based on flour as the reference point. Flour is always represented as 100% in the recipe. For example,
here is a reference recipe with both weights and percentages:
* 100% Flour - 500g
* 70% Water - 350g
* 2% Salt - 10g
* 0.6% yeast - 3g

## Hydration
Hydration refers to the amount of water in a recipe. You may hear terms like "high hydration" or "70% hydration". Here 70%
hydration refers to the bakers percentage of water in a recipe.

"High hydration" typically means above 80% hydration. High hydration doughs are more difficult to work with.

Beginners should opt on the lower end of the range.

## Leaven
> Leaven: A substance that causes dough or batter to rise

Common leavens include yeast, baking soda, and wild yeast.

"Natural Leaven" is made up of wild yeasts and bacteria that are cultivated from the air and from flour. They are typically
maintained as a Sourdough starter

### Sourdough Starter
A quick aside for sourdough starter. Sourdough starter is a continuous and living cultivation of wild yeast and bacteria.

The yeast are what ferments the flour producing carbon dioxide and ethanol. The carbon dioxide is caught in the dough producing
bubbles. When baked the hot air expands causing the bread to rise.

Making a starter: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZHm1aKxAsIs&t=15s

Once made, the starter needs to be maintained using regular feedings where a small portion of the starter is kept and the rest is discarded:
* 25g of old starter
* 50g of flour (I like 50/50 whole wheat and all purpose mix)
* 50g of water

### Natural Leaven
The starter isn't actually what is used to put in the dough and start the fermentation process. It is just the "starter".
Instead we make a separate leaven made slightly differently

My basic leaven:
* 25g starter at peak ripeness (8-12 hours after last feeding depending on temperature)
* 100g Bread flour
* 90g water

This leaven should be used 8-12 hours after making in its peak ripeness. The leaven should smell a little like yoghurt at its peak.

## Gluten
Gluten is instrumenal to making dough and you need to "develop" the gluten and build the gluten "network"

> Gluten: Protein in flour made up of glutenin and gliadin.

When flour is mixed with water, the glutenin and gliadin are able to move freely and tangle up with each other. This is how gluten
is formed. To have strong gluten development, the dough needs to have some sort of physical agitation. This is why dough is kneaded.
For higher hydration dough, stretch and folds are often easier to do than kneading. Stretch and folds: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mwtTZK7_t08

### Autolyse
> **Autolyse**: Period of time where flour and water are mixed to allow for gluten development

An autolyse can be used to start building a strong gluten network before the leavener starts disrupting. It is optional.
30 minutes is sufficient. To begin the autolyse, the water and flour need to be mixed to be homogenous but not
needed to build the gluten network. 5-10% of the total water should be reserved for mixing in the salt later.

### Stretch an folds
If using the stretch and fold technique, you should perform 3+ sets of stretch and folds (~8 folds each time) between 30 minutes
and an hour apart. But the end you should be able to perform the window pane test without tearing.

### Gluten in different flours
Gluten is present in many grains naturally but varies between different types of grain (spelt vs wheat), and different breeds
of a single type of grain (hard red wheat vs durum wheat)

In the US, here are basic flour types by gluten content:
* Pastry flour -> 8-10%
* All Purpose -> ~11%
* Bread Flour -> 12-14%
* Whole wheat -> 13-14%

Despite having a higher protein content, whole wheat typically develops a weaker gluten network since it has large chunks of
wheat hull or germ that can disrupt the network.

## Bulk Fermentation
After the autolyse, you mix in the leaven. To mix in, put the leaven on top of the dough. You can perform stretch and folds
to get the leaven to mix into the dough.

It may not mix fully initially. Leave it for a few minutes, allowing the gluten to relax. Then come back and perform more mixing.

It may also be useful to mash the dough with your wet hand.

## Shaping
Shaping the dough is the hardest part for me and the part that I have yet to figure out. You must be careful to not destroy all of
the bubbles. 

#### Lamination. 
Lamination is the process of flattening the dough and then layering it on itself. Put the dough on the counter. Stretch out into 
a flat square sheet.

Fold the dough into thirds. You should now have a long rectagle.

#### Rolling
Roll the dough down the long rectangle. You should have a tight spiral.

#### Corner folds
With the seam side of the roll facing you. Grab corners and pull them into the middle. You should do this around the shape of the loaf.
Approximately 8 corner folds.

## Baking
To bake bread you typically want to bake bread in 2 stages:
1. "Rising" - Baked at a high temperature with steam and humidity
2. "Crusting" - Baked at a lower temperature without steam

It isn't easy to introduce steam in a normal oven without a crazy apparatus. That's why dutch ovens with lids are useful.
A closed environment allows the water in the bread to produce the steam and allow the bread to rise without the crust
forming and hardening. Removing the lid allows the baking to enter the second phase.

The dutch oven should be preheated for at least 45 minutes at 475 F.

Bake for 20 minutes with lid on in dutch oven at 475. Remove lid and bake for another 20 minutes somewhere between 425 and 450.

## Recipe
* 80% All Purpose Flour
* 20% Whole Wheat
* 70% Hydration
* 1.8% Salt
* 11% Leaven

Either do 500g of total flour for one loaf or 1000g for two loaves. Store sliced in freezer.

To store at room temp, easiest way is face down on a cutting board.
